01 Workshop overview

Advancing autonomy across the space domain.

The program is organized around three themes spanning theory, computation, and space applications.

This workshop brings researchers together to share key challenges, recent advances, and emerging applications in guidance, navigation and control (GN&C) and autonomy across missions spanning low Earth orbit, cislunar and lunar operations, and deep space.

Presentations by leading researchers will address stochastic and nonlinear planning and control, optimization-based safe and resilient trajectory generation, uncertainty quantification and propagation, ML/AI for collaborative operations, and decentralized multi-spacecraft coordination and control.

Emphasis will span theoretical foundations, computational methods, simulation-based validation, and applications including mission design, orbit determination, attitude control, station keeping, maneuver transfer, rendezvous and proximity operations, and space domain awareness.

◎Designed for the research community. The program welcomes researchers, academics, graduate students, and participants new to space dynamics and control.

9:00–9:30 AMThe Lie Group Manifold Structure for Uncertainty Propagation for Spacecraft GNC ApplicationsPanagiotis TsiotrasGeorgia Tech
Talk details+

Abstract

Uncertainty propagation for rigid-body motion is a core task in aerospace guidance, navigation, and control (GNC), as well as in robotics. In aerospace applications, it supports rapid trajectory dispersion analysis, where trajectory uncertainty is approximated efficiently enough to assess closed-loop performance metrics such as landing footprint and mission robustness, and to support sensor and guidance architecture trade studies. While the manifold structure of rigid-body motion (e.g., SO(3) and SE(3)) is well studied, this additional structure is not treated consistently in stochastic settings in the GNC literature. The choice of Lie group structure becomes especially important for uncertainty propagation, where the group geometry directly influences how uncertainty evolves. I will present recent results on developing this structure directly within an abstract Lie-theoretic framework, without relying on the impractical matrix-group embedding of the left-trivialized TSE(3). The resulting formulation is both geometrically intrinsic and practically efficient for uncertainty propagation and control on the rigid-body velocity phase space. Through two lunar-landing numerical experiments, we demonstrate that the proposed group geometry admits a more accurate Gaussian approximation of the state distribution in its exponential coordinates than the direct-product formulation, and that it yields significantly better uncertainty propagation and filtering performance than direct-product formulations and linear covariance analysis approximations (e.g., EKF and M-EKF).

11:00–11:30 AMAI for Distributed Space SystemsSimone D’AmicoStanford University
Talk details+

Abstract

Distributed space systems are transforming spaceflight by replacing single monolithic spacecraft with multiple coordinated assets such as formations, swarms, and servicing vehicles that can observe, assemble, inspect, and maneuver cooperatively. Yet these missions raise the bar for onboard autonomy in order to be technically feasible and financially viable: spacecraft must perceive, decide, and act safely in uncertain, dynamic, and resource-constrained environments, often with limited ground intervention. This keynote will discuss frontier research on how to achieve these objectives through the judicious integration of artificial intelligence or learning-based approaches into the spacecraft autonomy stack to enhance rather than replace optimization-based guidance, navigation, and control. The talk will highlight the most recent advances obtained at Stanford in leveraging multimodal transformers for vision-based perception and navigation for unknown, noncooperative targets, semantically consistent trajectory generation for rendezvous and proximity operations, and autonomous reasoning for spacecraft guidance. Ultimately, this work marks an evolutionary step toward vision-based, language-conditioned, constraint-aware, and optimal spacecraft trajectory generation, enabling operators to shape both mission intent and safety-critical behavior through intuitive natural-language commands while reducing expert burden across future science, commercial, and defense missions.

Although dual quaternion guidance (DQG) has demonstrated real-time performance on flight hardware, it still faces some theoretical challenges that hinder its performance. 2:30–3:00 PMOptimization-Based Safe and Resilient Trajectory Generation for Space ApplicationsPurnanand ElangoMERL
Talk details+

Abstract

Future space missions require trajectory generation algorithms that ensure safety while remaining resilient to uncertainty, contingencies, and changing mission conditions. This talk presents a unified optimization-based perspective built on two complementary approaches: (i) continuous-time successive convexification, which promotes safety by enforcing dynamics and operational constraints throughout the trajectory rather than only at discrete sampling points; and (ii) deferred-decision trajectory optimization, which promotes resilience by preserving flexibility and postponing consequential decisions until additional information becomes available, thereby protecting against unmodeled uncertainty and unforeseen events. Together, these methods provide a foundation for reliable and adaptive trajectory generation for autonomous spacecraft guidance, navigation, and control.

3:00–3:30 PMConstrained Control in Cislunar DomainIlya KolmanovskyUniversity of Michigan
Talk details+

Abstract

The talk will highlight constrained-control problems in the cislunar domain, where spacecraft trajectories evolve according to solutions of the three-body problem encompassing Earth, Moon, and spacecraft. The focus will be on feedback stabilization and constrained control, including the application of reference governors and Model Predictive Control (MPC) to safe and fuel-efficient periodic-orbit tracking, rendezvous and proximity maneuvering near such orbits, and orbital transfers between such orbits. In particular, a variant of the reference governor, referred to as the time shift governor, will be presented. It commands a time-shifted trajectory of the chief spacecraft to the deputy spacecraft's controller, with the time shift computed to enforce safety constraints.
