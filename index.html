01 Workshop overview

Advancing autonomy across the space domain.

The program is organized around three themes spanning theory, computation, and space applications.

This workshop brings researchers together to share key challenges, recent advances, and emerging applications in guidance, navigation and control (GN&C) and autonomy across missions spanning low Earth orbit, cislunar and lunar operations, and deep space.

Presentations by leading researchers will address stochastic and nonlinear planning and control, optimization-based safe and resilient trajectory generation, uncertainty quantification and propagation, ML/AI for collaborative operations, and decentralized multi-spacecraft coordination and control.

Emphasis will span theoretical foundations, computational methods, simulation-based validation, and applications including mission design, orbit determination, attitude control, station keeping, maneuver transfer, rendezvous and proximity operations, and space domain awareness.

◎Designed for the research community. The program welcomes researchers, academics, graduate students, and participants new to space dynamics and control.

02 Research program

Core research
themes.

The program links theoretical foundations, computational methods, and representative mission applications across the space domain.

01

Planning and control

Risk-aware trajectory optimization

Stochastic and nonlinear planning, safe and resilient trajectory generation, and principled uncertainty quantification for demanding mission regimes.

  • Stochastic control
  • Nonlinear planning
  • Uncertainty propagation
02

Learning-enabled autonomy

Autonomy & learning-based techniques

Learning-enabled guidance, navigation, and control; AI for collaborative operations; and autonomy that can act safely beyond continuous ground contact.

  • Learning-based GNC
  • Collaborative AI
  • Safe autonomy
03

Distributed systems

Multi-spacecraft coordination & control

Decentralized coordination, formation flight, rendezvous, and resilient control for distributed missions from Earth orbit to cislunar space.

  • Distributed control
  • Formation flight
  • Proximity operations

Mission continuum

Applications across mission regimes.

Theory and computation meet mission design, orbit determination, navigation and attitude control, station keeping, transfer maneuvers, rendezvous and proximity operations, and space domain awareness.
